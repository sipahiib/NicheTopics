# Research and editorial notes

## Verified incidents

1. **Pizza glue (May 2024):** Google's [AI Overviews update](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/ai-overviews-update-may-2024/) acknowledges that sarcastic or troll content in discussion forums led to unhelpful advice, including using glue to make cheese stick to pizza.
2. **Eating rocks (May 2024):** The same [Google explanation](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/ai-overviews-update-may-2024/) discusses the question, the lack of high-quality serious sources, and a satirical source that appeared in an AI Overview. The video does not repeat any particular purported dosage.
3. **Mata v. Avianca (June 2023):** The [court's sanctions opinion](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Mata-v-Avianca-Inc.pdf) finds that lawyers submitted non-existent decisions and fake quotations created by ChatGPT, continued to defend them after questions arose, and were jointly sanctioned with a $5,000 penalty. The video keeps the responsibility with the lawyers, rather than saying the software itself was fined.

The video uses original illustrative graphics. It does not recreate alleged screenshots as if they were primary evidence. Google noted that many circulating screenshots were fabricated, so its own acknowledgement is the basis for the first two examples.

## Editorial structure

The pizza and rock examples open with the comic angle. The court incident shifts to real-world consequences. The final section offers a practical source-checking habit. This avoids treating a legal proceeding as a joke.
